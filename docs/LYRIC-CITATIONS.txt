1. Turing machine (Historical)
Turing’s tape: the rules encoded;
Turing describes symbolic computation using a tape, a finite machine state, and deterministic transition rules. His universal machine can read an encoded machine description.

2. Stored-program computing (Historical)
Von Neumann’s draft: instructions loaded.
Von Neumann's EDVAC draft describes a computer organization in which instructions are represented in memory. The line does not imply that he alone invented stored-program computing.

3. Dartmouth and AI winters (Historical + metaphor)
Dartmouth summer, winter snow;
The 1955 proposal led to the summer 1956 Dartmouth project. Winter snow is a metaphor for subsequent disillusionment and funding retrenchment; Lighthill's critical report and McCarthy's response are historical primary documents for that context.
[3] John McCarthy, Marvin Minsky, Nathaniel Rochester, Claude Shannon (1955-08-31; proposed meeting summer 1956). A Proposal for the Dartmouth Summer Research Project on Artificial Intelligence. https://www-formal.stanford.edu/jmc/history/dartmouth/dartmouth.html

4. Backpropagation (Historical)
Backprop found which way to go.
The influential 1986 Rumelhart-Hinton-Williams paper trains representations by propagating errors backward to compute weight updates. Which way to go evokes the gradient. This is a seminal popularization, not a claim that backpropagation had no earlier history.
[6] David E. Rumelhart, Geoffrey E. Hinton, Ronald J. Williams (1986). Learning representations by back-propagating errors. https://doi.org/10.1038/323533a0

AlexNet is the 2012 ImageNet convolutional-network breakthrough. AlphaGo defeated Lee Sedol 4-1 in March 2016; the celebrated move 37 occurred in game two. It is distinct from Lee Sedol's move 78 in game four.

34. Jacobian conjecture; Fable; Keller (Reported research result)
Three dimensions: Jacobian falls;
Fable finds cracks in Keller’s walls.

36. Unit distances and algebraic number fields (Reported research result)

42. I. J. Good and intelligence explosion (Explicit historical intellectual reference)
I. J. Good’s old feedback game:
